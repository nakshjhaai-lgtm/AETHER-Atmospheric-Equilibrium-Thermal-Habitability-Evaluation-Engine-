The Mathematical Core of AETHER

Client-side radiative transfer & similarity indexing. All models below are simplified educational approximations, not validated research instruments.

01
Equilibrium Thermal Gradient

Teq = Teff · √( R★ / 2d ) · (1 − A)¼

Bond albedo-bound blackbody equilibrium at orbital distance d, before optical-depth correction.

Teff: Effective stellar temperature (K)
R★: Stellar radius (solar radii, converted to km internally)
d: Orbital distance (AU, converted to km internally)
A: Bond albedo (dimensionless, 0–1)

Assumptions: Planet is a blackbody. Star emits as a perfect blackbody. Albedo is uniform across all wavelengths. No atmospheric effects at this stage.

02
Eddington Approximation: Surface Temperature

Ts4 = (¾) Teq4 · ( τs + ⅔ )

τ (optical depth) is a dimensionless measure of how strongly the atmosphere absorbs outgoing infrared radiation. This implementation treats the atmosphere as a grey, plane-parallel layer and does not model individual gases.

Ts: Surface temperature (K)
Teq: Equilibrium temperature from Eq. 01 (K)
τs: Surface optical depth (dimensionless, 0–15)

Assumptions: Uses the Hopf function approximation q(τ) ≈ 2/3. Atmosphere is "grey" (absorbs all wavelengths equally). Does not model convection, clouds, or specific greenhouse gases. At very high τ, the model becomes unreliable.

03
Bulk Density & Escape Velocity

ρ = Mp / (4/3 π Rp³)    ve = √(2GMp/Rp)

Derived from mass-radius inputs. Core-material preset (iron/silicate/water) scales effective density relative to Earth's 5.51 g/cm³.

Mp: Planetary mass (Earth masses)
Rp: Planetary radius (Earth radii)
G: Gravitational constant (6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²)
ρ: Bulk density (g/cm³)
ve: Escape velocity (km/s)

Assumptions: Planet is a uniform sphere. Core-material multiplier is a rough approximation of compositional effects.

04
Geometric-Mean Similarity Heuristic

SI = ( SR · Sρ · Sv · ST )¼

A custom weighted geometric mean comparing a planet's radius, density, escape velocity, and temperature against reference baselines (Earth, Mars, Venus). This is an exploratory heuristic: it is not a probability of life, not a validated habitability metric, and the weights are model assumptions.

SR: Radius sub-index (Bray-Curtis-like symmetric deviation)
Sρ: Density sub-index
Sv: Escape velocity sub-index
ST: Temperature sub-index

Similarity to Earth does not imply habitability. Similarity to Mars or Venus is not a classification. Weights are hand-tuned, not empirically derived.

05
Kopparapu Effective Stellar Flux Boundaries

Seff = Seff☉ + aT★ + bT★² + cT★³ + dT★⁴

4th-order polynomial boundaries defining habitable-zone edges (Recent Venus → Early Mars). T★ = Teff − 5780 K. Evaluated per 1 M⊕ benchmark. The resulting Seff is converted to an orbital distance via d = √(L/Seff).

Seff: Effective stellar flux (dimensionless, relative to Earth)
Seff☉: Solar-effective flux at boundary (from Kopparapu table)
a, b, c, d: Polynomial coefficients (from Kopparapu et al. 2013)
L: Stellar luminosity (solar luminosities)

Validated for 1 M⊕ planets. The 4th-order polynomial becomes unreliable far outside the 2500–7000 K stellar temperature range.

Results are educational approximations, not predictive science.
